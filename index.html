About

Cloud processes across scales

I study how microphysical processes within clouds shape precipitation, deep convection, weather, and climate.

I am a postdoctoral researcher at the Institute of Meteorology and Climate Research (IMKTRO), Karlsruhe Institute of Technology (KIT), Germany. My current work focuses on mixed-phase and ice-cloud processes in the ICON modeling framework, including aerosol–cloud interactions and kilometer-scale atmospheric simulations.

My PhD research at Lund University focused on primary and secondary ice formation in mixed-phase and deep convective clouds, aerosol influences on cloud microphysics, and the consequences of these processes for cloud evolution and radiative effects.

I am extending this cloud-physics foundation toward extreme convective weather and atmospheric electricity, with particular interest in the coupling between cloud microphysics, thunderstorm charging, and lightning.

Research Journey

From ice microphysics to electrified storms

A progression from process-level cloud physics toward questions connecting microphysics, convection, extreme weather, and lightning.

2019–2023

PhD · Lund University

Cloud microphysics, aerosol–cloud interactions, primary ice nucleation and secondary ice production in mixed-phase and deep convective clouds using detailed numerical modeling.

2023–present

Postdoctoral research · KIT

Representation and evaluation of mixed-phase and ice-cloud processes in ICON, with emphasis on kilometer-scale atmospheric modeling and aerosol coupling.

Current

Connecting microphysics to convection

Investigating how ice-production processes influence the microphysics and dynamics of deep convective clouds across contrasting environments.

Emerging

Extreme weather & atmospheric electricity

Building an independent direction around thunderstorm electrification and lightning, with cloud microphysics providing the physical bridge between aerosols, hydrometeor interactions, charge separation, and convective storms.
